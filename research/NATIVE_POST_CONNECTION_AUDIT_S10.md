# S10 — Native post-connection failure audit (S8-C, diagnostic only)

**Answer in one paragraph.** S8-C builds its first connection early and keeps delivering until it dies: in the 20 S8-C S9 episodes that delivered at all, the last delivery came a median 4 decisions before game over, so the policy does not "stop"; it is outgrown by a city that spawns houses faster than it connects them. In the 4 measurement-only native replays, every episode contains long windows in which a house is unconnected, the native budget would have paid for the missing route, and the policy was instead **replacing house edges** (replacement ADDs are 52 %, 93 %, 78 % and 16 % of ADDs; in the last ~50 decisions of the two long episodes 33 and 21 connection-loss events were caused by such swaps). A real, separate constraint also exists: the road budget (concrete) was exhausted in 3/4 episodes (33–45 % of decisions at zero) and refills only weekly. **Dominant obstacle (ranked on the available evidence, n = 4 measured + 24 proxy-based): non-productive house-edge churn that destroys or delays connections while resources and a short route are available; resource exhaustion from over-building is the second constraint.** The missing −10 terminal penalty is real but is not what limits the policy. Recommended single next step: *reduce destructive road churn*, starting with a cheap causal probe (§11), not native score fine-tuning.
Nothing was trained, no reward/mask/checkpoint/synthetic-environment change, no anti-cycle filter. Native wall-clock used: **2.6 min** (36 s instrumentation twin + 4 parallel replays of 46–64 s; cap 10 min).

## 8. Terminal reward audit (no reward changed)
* **Frequency.** S9: 2/48 episodes lacked the −10 (0/24 S1, 2/24 S8-C: p2det 571271 at decision 392 and p2sto 500000 at decision 315); terminated episodes: 46 with the penalty, 2 without. S4: 5/20, S6 paired runs 0/4, S10 replays 0/4 (all four last rewards −10). Pooled across the native normal-start runs that logged the flag: **7/76 ≈ 9 %**.
* **Native transition.** Both S9 cases show the S5 mechanism exactly: the final STEP starts with the game already terminal (`applyInfo = "terminal state (not applied)"`, `accepted = false`, penalty 0) and the clock advanced 0.1 s instead of 1.0 s; the 46 normal terminal steps all advanced 1.0 s. The wall-clock gap before the final step does not separate them (22 of 46 normal cases had < 0.1 s), so timing is not an explanation; the source of the 0.1 s tick remains unresolved (S5).
* **Effect on PPO.** The penalty is the only negative signal, and policy-independent in non-delivering episodes. A missing −10 changes the terminal reward from −10 to 0 in ≈ 9 % of episodes: bias ≈ +0.9 per episode and a standard deviation ≈ 3 on the terminal reward. For S8-C-type policies, returns are dominated by deliveries (+40…+146), so the effect is small; for weak policies with few deliveries (early training, random init) it removes up to a quarter of the only signal (S4: 25 %). It is therefore a correctness issue to fix before native PPO but **not the obstacle limiting S8-C**.
* **Proposed fix (not implemented; needs approval).** Transition-level rule in the STEP handler / wrapper: pay −10 on the **first transition that reports `terminated = true`** (track a per-episode "terminal already reported" flag, reset on RESET) instead of requiring the game to turn terminal *during* that step; never on truncation; never twice. Python-side equivalent: if `terminated`, `terminalPenalty == 0` and no earlier terminated report, add −10 and set `info["penalty_injected"]`. Regression tests: (1) replay of saved terminal-on-entry traces (S5 `hunt_*`, S9 rows 571271 / 500000) must yield reward −10 exactly once at the final transition; (2) normal terminal traces unchanged (−10 once); (3) horizon-truncated episodes receive no penalty; (4) after a terminated response no further step reward is emitted; (5) a paired logging-on/off rollout is identical. Historical S9 returns are not rewritten.

## 9. Ranked bottlenecks
1. **Non-productive and destructive house-edge churn** (F3) — observed in 4/4 replays and in 20/24 S9 episodes by the replacement-share criterion; present *while* a short route and sufficient concrete existed in every replay; produces connection-loss events (54 in two episodes). Confidence: high that it is a major contributor, moderate that it is *the* dominant one (n = 4, inference about the final collapse).
2. **Road-budget exhaustion from early over-building** (F1) — observed in 3/4 replays (33–45 % of decisions at zero); proxy suggests 19/24 S9 episodes; blocks new houses that spawn at zero concrete and refills only weekly. Confidence: high that it binds; the over-building cause is inferred.
3. **Spawn adaptation / distance** (F2) — unconnected houses at the end in 21/24; some new houses need 9–11 edges (beyond the remaining budget). Partly a consequence of 1 and 2.
4. **Late first connection** (F5) — 4/24, consequence of the same churn plus budget timing.
5. **Terminal-penalty inconsistency** — ≈ 9 %, relevant for learning, not for the observed failure.
6. **Throughput / congestion** (F4) — unverified.

## 10. Decision gate
**Dominant obstacle:** the policy repeatedly spends its decisions replacing house edges (including destroying finished connections) instead of building the missing short routes, and the road budget it has not conserved cannot be re-spent until the next weekly top-up; the city outgrows its connected part and a destination overcrowds. Distinguish the immediate mechanism (destination overcrowding → game over) from the upstream contributors above.

## 11. Recommendation (exactly one primary step): reduce destructive road churn
Smallest decisive experiment (inference only, no training, ≈ 7 min native): replay the same 12 S9 seeds with S8-C stochastic under an **evaluation-only restriction** that removes from the native mask the replacement ADDs that touch an already-connected house (privileged, clearly labelled as an intervention, native mask otherwise untouched), and compare connections lost, deliveries, unconnected houses at the end, survival and concrete use with the unrestricted S9 run. If deliveries / survival rise substantially, churn is causally dominant and the training intervention is to teach churn avoidance (e.g. a synthetic continuation of S8-C in an S7 extension with several spawning houses, continuing deliveries and weekly concrete top-ups, where a broken connection naturally costs deliveries — no reward shaping); if not, budget exhaustion and spawn adaptation dominate and the next step becomes resource-efficient construction.
**Why this before fine-tuning on native score:** (a) native PPO costs ≈ 1–1.5 h per 10k decisions and the churn signal is delayed over hundreds of decisions, so it would be an expensive way to discover which behaviour to fix; (b) the evidence ranks behaviours, but only the restriction test separates churn from budget causally; (c) the native reward still carries the ≈ 9 % missing-penalty noise that should be corrected (with your approval) before any reward-driven native learning; (d) S8 showed that sparse-reward learning works in synthetic worlds at 40 decisions/s with controllable difficulty, so the cheaper place to teach a behaviour is a synthetic world that finally contains the missing mechanics (multiple houses, weekly budget).
