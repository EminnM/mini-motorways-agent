## 1. The navy-blue case (seed 1007922, colour group 3)
Reconstruction: headless B+ is deterministic, so the state is reproducible decision-for-decision, but the real-time-paced visible run has a different spawn sequence after the first clock snap, so the headless replay reproduces the *detour geometry*, not the screenshot's congestion state. Native lane paths are not exported; driving distances are BFS over the active road graph (the S25-validated static-router proxy); traffic is `visibleVehicleCount` per tile.
* Which houses: (−5,−1) and (−5,0) by the river, destination block (−13,−1)…(−12,0) in the south-west.
* Their route: 22 / 21 tiles via the northern corridor (through (−5,2)…(−8,6)…(−14,4)), while the shortest legal path is 10 / 9 tiles for 8 new tiles, breaks nothing, and is affordable from decision ~313 (39–43 roads in stock). Detour ratio 2.2–2.3; the corridor is shared with three other colours.
* Why planner B/B+ never built it: the route search is concrete-first lexicographic and never re-optimises a connected house (`PLANNER_GEOMETRY_S35.md`).
* Does navy-blue actually suffer? In the B+ replay its pins creep 2 → 3 → 6 over decisions 400–900 and it **overcrowds at 906**; the game ends at 964 with navy (7 pins) and colour 1 (8) overcrowded. The screenshot's diagnosis holds.

## 2. The intervention and whether cars use it
Planner-generated bypass (`bypass=True`, geometry gate only at that time): at decision 323 the planner adopts `house (−5,0): drive 21 → 9, cost 8, score +12.0 − 4.8`, builds the 9 edges over the next ~10 decisions (0 illegal, connectivity intact). Afterwards the house's static route is **6 tiles, entirely on the new road** (it touches the destination block directly). Cars: 0 on the new tiles for ~170 decisions (navy demand ≈ 0–1, cars stay home), then 0.16–0.36 vehicles per sample from decision 500 (max 2) — they use it when they drive; navy traffic is simply light (2 houses).

## 4. Generalisation attempt (Phase 4) and its two revisions
* v1 — geometry-only rule (detour ≥ 2×, ≤ 10 tiles, affordable, breaks nothing), all colours: 10 of 12 new seeds ran before being stopped at the user's objection that it spends tiles uninformedly. Result: on 9 of 10 seeds **no bypass fired** and the outcome is identical to B+; on 1171274 two bypasses fired (colour 3: 17→7 for 6 tiles at 395; colour 1: 19→8 for 7 at 519) and the episode went 368@910 → 408@940 (+40); on 1163355 the outcome differed (472@1072 → 205@713) with no bypass recorded — unexplained (native transient or an unlogged plan); kept as a labelled control in `eval/new_geometry_only_bypass/`.
* v2 — demand gate added (`bypass_min_demand = 3`: a colour is eligible only with ≥ 3 pins waiting or an overcrowded destination): 3 of 12 seeds ran before the stop, **no bypass fired**, outcomes identical to B+.
* The user then objected that the gate is rigid and the policy deterministic. Proposed (not implemented): replace thresholds by a continuous expected-benefit score (pins waiting × tiles saved per trip vs tiles × scarcity), and, if headroom is shown, make "build this bypass / don't" the decision point of a small learned policy (observation: pins, tiles saved, cost, scarcity, junction load; reward: native deliveries). Not started.

## 5. Decision
**INCONCLUSIVE.** Established: the mechanism works end to end (detects the detour the screenshot shows, builds a legal affordable shortcut, cars use it, the targeted colour stops accumulating pins). Not established: whether this improves native outcomes — one seed with confounded divergence, and in 12–13 further seeds the rule fired on only one (+40). What would resolve it: the continuous-score version on the 12 new seeds plus the 8 fresh seeds (≤ 20 episodes, ≈ 25 min on 2 instances), reported per colour (pin curves, first overcrowding) and per episode (deliveries, survival, tiles), against the existing B+ episodes. No RL was trained.
